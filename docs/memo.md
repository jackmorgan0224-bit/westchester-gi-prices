# Memo: GI endoscopy prices at Westchester hospitals

**To:** a managed care or network strategy team
**Re:** what the 2026 hospital price files show for colonoscopy, EGD, and EUS in Westchester County
**Data:** 10 hospital price files downloaded Oct 25, 2026; 9,903 negotiated dollar rates; Medicare OPPS benchmark (Oct 2026, wage-adjusted)

## Bottom line

For a commercial plan, the price of a routine colonoscopy in Westchester depends far more on **which hospital** and **which insurer contract** than on the procedure itself. The median commercial case rate is about 4x Medicare, but it runs from roughly 1x at St. John's Riverside to nearly 6x at NYP Westchester and White Plains Hospital. Government-linked business (Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, Essential Plan) clusters tightly around Medicare rates.

## 1. Line of business

| Diagnostic colonoscopy (45378) | Median rate | Multiple of Medicare | Middle half |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial (case rates) | $4,724 | 4.1x | 2.7x to 5.8x |
| Exchange (individual market) | $1,637 | 1.4x | 1.1x to 2.6x |
| NY Essential Plan | $1,602 | 1.4x | 0.9x to 2.0x |
| Medicare Advantage | $1,155 | 1.0x | 0.9x to 1.0x |
| Medicaid managed care | $959 | 0.8x | 0.7x to 0.9x |

Medicare for this code at these hospitals: **$1,161**. Of the five segments in this table, commercial pays the most and Medicaid the least for every one of the 11 codes studied. Medicare Advantage rates sit within a few percent of traditional Medicare almost everywhere, consistent with contracts priced at or near 100% of Medicare. Medicaid pays least for the complex procedures: **0.37x** Medicare for an upper EUS.

The two Northwell hospitals publish identical commercial rates, consistent with system-level contracting. The spread within a hospital is also wide: at Montefiore New Rochelle the same procedure ranges from $3,549 to $18,107 depending on the plan.

**So what:** for a plan steering members to lower-cost sites, moving a colonoscopy from a ~$6,500 hospital to a ~$3,100 one saves more than the entire Medicare payment for the procedure.

**So what:** if I were renegotiating for a hospital, UnitedHealthcare's rates are the ones to protect and MVP's the ones to raise. If I were an employer choosing a carrier, the same procedure at the same hospital costs about 50% more through UnitedHealthcare than through the typical plan.

## 4. Flat case rates

Medicare pays more for harder procedures: $1,161 for a diagnostic colonoscopy, $1,494 for one with polyp removal (+29%), and $2,395 for an upper EUS. Commercial contracts often don't. **84 of 113** commercial plans pay exactly the same case rate for a diagnostic colonoscopy and a polyp removal, and at Phelps and Northern Westchester the median commercial case rate is the same $3,352 for an EGD with biopsy, a polyp removal, and an upper EUS.

**So what:** commercial "multiples of Medicare" look lower for EUS (2.8x) than for colonoscopy (4.1x) not because EUS is a better deal for the hospital per se, but because many contracts price every endoscopy visit the same. A hospital doing more complex endoscopy under flat case rates is leaving money on the table relative to Medicare's relative weights.

## Questions I'd want answered before acting

1. Westchester Medical Center lists traditional Medicare at **$4,069** for a diagnostic colonoscopy, 3.5x Medicare's own published rate for that hospital ($1,161), while its EUS Medicare rows ($2,384) match Medicare within 0.5%. Is the colonoscopy figure a data error?
2. White Plains Hospital and Montefiore Mount Vernon publish two different prices for the same plan and code: a case rate and a much lower single-line fee schedule (median 5.6x and 4.2x apart). Which one does a patient's cost estimate use?
3. Are the "percent of billed charges" contracts (Emblem, MultiPlan, PHCS at Westchester Medical Center) applied to the gross charge of the whole visit? None of those rows publish a gross charge.
